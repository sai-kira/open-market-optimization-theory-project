## 📌 Executive Summary

The **Open Market Optimization Theory (OMOT)** is a formal economic research initiative investigating the relationship between household labor supply, competitive open markets, and non-elastic positional goods. 

A central economic belief of the past half-century has been that expanding household market labor—specifically the transition from single-earner to dual-earner households—yields a permanent increase in household living standards and financial autonomy.

**The OMOT Framework proves otherwise:** In competitive market economies containing goods with near-zero supply elasticity (primarily urban residential land, credentialing education, and healthcare), market prices clear based on aggregate household bidding capacity. Consequently, uniform expansions in household labor supply are capitalized into the costs of fixed positional necessities. Rather than creating permanent discretionary surplus, the market recalibrates the baseline cost of living to the dual-income ceiling, transforming two full-time incomes from an avenue of wealth into a structural requirement for basic subsistence.

## 🔬 Core Theoretical Architecture

The forthcoming working paper establishes a formal axiomatic general equilibrium model demonstrating:

1. **The Surplus Absorption Theorem:** Proof that when positional goods possess near-zero supply elasticity ($\epsilon_P \to 0$), increases in aggregate purchasing power resulting from expanded labor supply are asymptotically absorbed by positional rent extraction, leaving real discretionary surplus invariant.
2. **The Irreversibility Theorem (The $\alpha^*$ Boundary):** Mathematical derivation of a critical macro dual-earner penetration threshold (approximately $\alpha^* \approx 0.60$) beyond which unilateral household reversion to single-earner status guarantees structural insolvency at market prices.
3. **The Positional Ratchet & Time-Welfare Degradation:** Game-theoretic proof (Prisoner's Dilemma) demonstrating that while individual entry into the dual-earner workforce is a dominant personal strategy, the aggregate Nash Equilibrium results in society expending double the labor hours for an identical relative ranking in positional goods, producing net welfare destruction via severe time poverty.

## 📈 Multi-Century Empirical Verification

The OMOT framework is econometrically evaluated and corroborated using four authoritative, independent institutional databases spanning 150 years:

* **Jordà-Schularick-Taylor (JST) Macrohistory Database (1870–2020):** 150 continuous years of macroeconomic data across 18 advanced economies, documenting the systematic decoupling of housing asset prices from wage growth and the 5.4-fold expansion of household mortgage debt relative to GDP.
* **Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) & BLS (1947–2026):** 80 continuous years tracking the strong negative correlation ($r = -0.7408$) between women's labor force participation and personal saving rates, alongside divergent hyper-inflation in medical care and tuition relative to general CPI.
* **Eurostat Dissemination Database (2005–2025):** Official house price indices across 38 European countries verifying persistent real-estate cost escalation outpacing median wage trends.
* **The World Bank Institutional Panel (1970–2023):** 53 years of demographic and labor indicators across major Asian and global economies, identifying the fertility collapse signature resulting from unfinanceable child-rearing costs in dual-earner urban centers.
